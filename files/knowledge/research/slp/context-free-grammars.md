# Context-Free Grammars

## Extrait
CHAPTER 18 • CONTEXT-FREE GRAMMARS AND CONSTITUENCY PARSING Syntactic constituency is the idea that groups of words can behave as single units, or constituents. Part of developing a grammar involves building an inventory of the constituents in the language. How do words group together in English? Consider the noun phrase, a sequence of words surrounding at least one noun. Here are some noun phrase examples of noun phrases (thanks to Damon Runyon): Harry the Horse a high-class spot such as Mindy’s the Broadway coppers the reason he comes into the Hot Box they three parties from Brooklyn What evidence do we have that these words group together (or “form constituents”)? One piece of evidence is that they can all appear in similar syntactic environments, for example, before a verb. three parties from Brooklyn arrive... a high-class spot such as Mindy’s attracts... the Broadway coppers love... they sit But while the whole noun phrase can occur before a verb, this is not true of each of the individual words that make up a noun phrase.
