[Between each word in a sentence and its neighbors, the mind perceives connections. These connections together form the scaffolding of the sentence.] Lucien Tesni`ere. 1959. ´El´ements de syntaxe structurale, A.1.§3 The focus of the last chapter was on context-free grammars and constituentbased representations. Here we present another important family of grammar formalisms called dependency grammars. In dependency formalisms, phrasal condependency grammars stituents and phrase-structure rules do not play a direct role. Instead, the syntactic structure of a sentence is described solely in terms of directed binary grammatical relations between the words, as in the following dependency parse: I prefer the morning flight through Denver nsubj obj det compound nmod case root (19.1) Relations among the words are illustrated above the sentence with directed, labeled arcs from heads to dependents. We call this a typed dependency structure because typed dependency the labels are drawn from a fixed inventory of grammatical relations.
